Our honest read on basalt vs anorthosite

  • Our model cannot tell how much of each ingredient to use. It was trained on lunar samples, and nothing in our data says what the suppliers' anorthosite or glass-rich basalt contain.
  • We tried to infer their make-up from the sheets' own chemistry. The model flags both sheets as low confidence (Na2O is outside the lunar training range; simulants are not lunar samples) and the inference came out inconsistent (an 'anorthosite' with about 23% glass, a 'basalt' that was about 46% olivine), so it is not used.
  • What the model does support are limits that follow from the ideal recipe: glass-rich basalt must be at least the glass line (it is the only glass source), anorthosite (more than 90% plagioclase) at most the plagioclase line divided by 0.90, and bronzite, olivine and ilmenite at most their own lines.
  • Reading the result: the current LHS-1 sheet has less basalt and more anorthosite than the ideal allows, which fits the training soils carrying roughly 15-35% glass. The LMS-1 headline is basalt-dominated with almost no glass, so its limits are loose; the soils-only version is more informative.
  • TODO: the suppliers' modal mineralogy of their anorthosite and basalt would make the split exact.

AnorthositeA rock: by definition more than 90 % plagioclasePlagioclase (mostly)
Basalt (glass-rich basalt)A rock mixing plagioclase, pyroxene, ilmenite and, here, a lot of glassSeveral: plagioclase, pyroxene, glass, a little ilmenite (amounts not given on the sheets)
BronziteA single mineral: magnesium-rich orthopyroxenePyroxene
OlivineA single mineral: magnesium-iron silicateOlivine
IlmeniteA single mineral: iron-titanium oxide (FeTiO3)Ilmenite
AgglutinatesGlass-welded soil particles made by micrometeorite impacts, with tiny iron particlesGlass (one kind of it; neither sheet lists agglutinates)
